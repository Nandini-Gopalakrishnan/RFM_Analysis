# 📊 Customer Segmentation using RFM Analysis

An end-to-end customer segmentation project that uses **Python**, **MySQL**, and **Power BI** to identify high-value customers and generate actionable business insights using the **RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary)** framework.

---

## 📌 Project Overview

Customer segmentation helps businesses understand customer purchasing behavior and create personalized marketing strategies.

In this project, I analyzed an online retail dataset and built a complete analytics pipeline—from data cleaning and transformation to customer segmentation and dashboard visualization.

Using the RFM model, customers were classified into six business-friendly segments to support customer retention and targeted marketing decisions.

---

## 🎯 Business Problem

Online retail businesses often have thousands of customers with varying purchasing behaviors. Treating every customer the same can result in ineffective marketing campaigns, lower customer retention, and missed revenue opportunities.

This project addresses this challenge by using **RFM Analysis** to identify valuable customers, customers at risk of churn, and customers requiring targeted engagement.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Clean and prepare transactional retail data
- Calculate Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) metrics
- Segment customers based on purchasing behavior
- Build an interactive Power BI dashboard
- Generate actionable business insights and recommendations

## 🧹 Data Cleaning (Python)

The dataset was cleaned using **Pandas** before performing the RFM analysis.

Cleaning steps included:

- Removed missing values
- Removed invalid transactions
- Filtered Quantity > 0
- Filtered Unit Price > 0
- Removed the StockCode column
- Exported the cleaned dataset for SQL analysis

After preprocessing, the dataset contained **354,321 valid transactions**.

---

## 📈 RFM Analysis (MySQL)

RFM metrics were calculated for each customer:

### Recency

Days since the customer's most recent purchase.

### Frequency

Total number of unique orders placed.

### Monetary

Total revenue generated by each customer.

To score customers, **NTILE(3)** was used:

- Top 33% → Score = 1
- Middle 33% → Score = 2
- Bottom 33% → Score = 3

Customers were then classified into six segments using SQL CASE statements.

---

## 👥 Customer Segments

| Segment | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| 🏆 Champions | Purchased recently, buy frequently, and spend the most |
| ❤️ Loyal Customers | Frequent purchasers who consistently generate high value |
| 🌱 New Customers | Recently acquired customers with low purchase frequency |
| 🔍 Need Attention | Moderate purchasing behavior requiring engagement |
| ⚠️ At Risk | Previously valuable customers who have stopped purchasing recently |
| 😴 Lost Customers | Inactive customers with low spending and purchase frequency |

## 📈 Key Insights

- A total of **3,920 customers** were analysed.
- **Needs Attention** is the largest customer segment, representing **49%** of all customers.
- **Champions** account for **17%** of customers and generate the highest customer spending.
- **At Risk** customers represent only **2%** of the customer base.
- Among the Top 10 highest-spending customers:
  - 7 are Champions
  - 2 belong to Needs Attention
  - 1 is a Loyal Customer

These findings highlight opportunities to improve customer retention through targeted engagement strategies.

## 💡 Business Recommendations

- Reward **Champions** with loyalty programmes and exclusive offers.
- Re-engage **Needs Attention** customers through personalised marketing campaigns.
- Launch win-back campaigns for **At Risk** customers.
- Nurture **New Customers** with onboarding offers and personalised recommendations.
- Recover **Lost Customers** using targeted reactivation campaigns.
- Encourage repeat purchases through cross-selling and product recommendations.
